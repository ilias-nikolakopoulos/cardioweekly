Key takeaways

AHA Scientific Sessions 2026 closed on Monday, Nov 9. This issue covers the Nov 8–9 late-breakers that were not yet published when No. 6 went out, plus the rest of a thin week. Two items meet the practice-changing bar. No new society guideline with graded recommendations was found.

  • Practice-changing
    Left bundle-branch pacing beats RV pacing for AV block (OptimPacing). In 685 patients, death, HF hospitalization or pacing-induced cardiomyopathy at 4 years was 8.5% vs 19.2% (HR 0.41, 0.23–0.75; NNT about 9). Open-label and stopped early with 51 events, but LEAP-Block points the same way.
  • Practice-changing
    FDA approves asundexian (Sunharbo), the first factor XIa inhibitor. 50 mg once daily on top of antiplatelet therapy after non-cardioembolic ischaemic stroke or high-risk TIA: 26% fewer ischaemic strokes (−2.2 points) without more major bleeding. Not approved for atrial fibrillation.
  • Trial
    Survodutide is cardiovascularly safe, not yet beneficial (SYNCHRONIZE-CVOT). In 5,507 adults with obesity, MACE was 3.7% vs 4.1% (HR 0.89, 0.67–1.18; non-inferior). One in five stopped the drug for GI effects.
  • Trial
    Low-voltage-area ablation does not help all-comers with persistent AF (COAST-AF). Recurrence 49.4% vs 57.5% (HR 0.82, 0.65–1.04). In patients who actually had low-voltage areas it was 49.3% vs 69.2%, which is hypothesis-generating.
  • Trial
    A community and EMS bundle did not improve cardiac-arrest survival (RACE-CARS). Survival with good neurological outcome was 9.0% vs 9.4% across 22,459 arrests in North Carolina.
  • Trial
    Dapagliflozin slows eGFR decline after heart transplant (DAPARHT). +1.7 mL/min/1.73 m² over 12 months vs placebo in 430 recipients, with no excess infections.

NEJM · Nov 8, 2026 · PMID 43001763Practice-changing

OptimPacing: left bundle-branch pacing vs right ventricular pacing for AV block

Bottom lineFor patients with AV block who will pace the ventricle most of the time, left bundle-branch pacing should now be the default where operators are trained. LEAP-Block (No. 5) found the same direction and size of effect. Caveats: open-label, 51 events, early stopping (which tends to overstate effects), and a composite that includes pacing-induced cardiomyopathy, an imaging-defined outcome.

Design
Investigator-initiated, international (mainly China), open-label RCT; N=702 randomized, 685 analysed; median 35.1 months; stopped early at a prespecified interim analysis
Population
Atrioventricular block needing a pacemaker; mean age 68, 57.5% men
Comparison
Left bundle-branch pacing vs right ventricular pacing
Primary endpoint
Death, HF hospitalization or pacing-induced cardiomyopathy: 16 vs 35 patients; 4-year incidence 8.5% vs 19.2%, HR 0.41 (0.23–0.75); −10.7 points, NNT about 9
Safety
Serious adverse events unrelated to the implant procedure 11.8% vs 9.3% (p=0.32)
Lancet · Nov 9, 2026 · PMID 43003106

SYNCHRONIZE-CVOT: cardiovascular safety of survodutide in obesity

Bottom lineSurvodutide does not raise cardiovascular risk, which clears a regulatory hurdle, but with 212 events the trial cannot show benefit. For patients who need CV risk reduction, semaglutide and tirzepatide have the outcome data. GI tolerability is clearly worse than placebo. Funded by Boehringer Ingelheim.

Design
Phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled, event-driven safety trial; 524 sites in 34 countries; N=5,530 randomized (5,507 treated), 1:1:1; median 22 months
Population
BMI ≥27 with established CVD, or two or more obesity complications or CV risk factors, or CKD with BMI ≥30; 40% women
Comparison
Weekly SC survodutide (glucagon/GLP-1 dual agonist) 3.6 mg or 6.0 mg vs placebo
Primary endpoint
CV death, stroke, MI, ischaemia-driven coronary revascularization or HF event 3.7% vs 4.1%, HR 0.89 (0.67–1.18); non-inferior against a 1.65 margin, superiority not shown
Safety
Nausea 48–49% vs 12%; vomiting 32–34% vs 5%; stopped for GI events 19.3% and 20.8% vs 1.9%; serious AEs 22.5% and 20.8% vs 23.9%. Only 56–59% of each group were still on treatment at close-out
JAMA · Nov 8, 2026 · PMID 42999998

COAST-AF: low-voltage-area ablation added to PVI in persistent AF

Bottom lineRoutine low-voltage-area (LVA) ablation did not significantly reduce recurrence. It looked effective in the half of patients who had LVA and did nothing in those without. A reasonable selective strategy when LVA is found, but a subgroup of a neutral trial is not enough to make it standard.

Design
Single-blind RCT at 14 Canadian centres; N=508; mean follow-up 21 months
Population
Persistent AF for more than 3 months; mean age 65, 76% men; 54% had LVA on mapping
Comparison
PVI plus LVA ablation vs PVI alone
Primary endpoint
Atrial arrhythmia recurrence after 90 days, single procedure 49.4% vs 57.5%, HR 0.82 (0.65–1.04; p=0.11)
Subgroups
LVA present: 49.3% vs 69.2% (HR 0.77, 0.66–0.91). No LVA: 49.6% vs 45.1% (HR 1.09, 0.90–1.32)
Safety
Similar between groups; longer procedure and ablation times with LVA ablation
NEJM · Nov 8, 2026 · PMID 43001758

RACE-CARS: community and EMS interventions for out-of-hospital cardiac arrest

Bottom lineA well-resourced, multi-component community programme did not move survival. The components remain sound, but delivering them at this intensity was not enough; bystander CPR and AED rates in the full paper will show whether delivery or the concept fell short. Funded by NHLBI.

Design
Registry-based, cluster-randomized trial; 55 county clusters in North Carolina; N=22,459 arrests; July 2022 to June 2026
Population
Adults with EMS-treated out-of-hospital cardiac arrest; median age 64, 37% women
Comparison
Bundle (telecommunicator training in arrest recognition and CPR instructions, community CPR training, earlier AED use by first responders and bystanders) vs usual care
Primary endpoint
Survival to discharge with CPC 1–2 9.0% vs 9.4%; adjusted OR 0.97 (0.75–1.26; p=0.80)
NEJM · Nov 9, 2026 · PMID 43002172

TYDAL-TIMI 78: MAR001 (ANGPTL4 antibody) for elevated triglycerides

Bottom lineA new triglyceride and remnant-cholesterol target aimed at ASCVD risk, not pancreatitis. Lipid endpoint only at 12 weeks; nothing to act on until an outcomes trial. Funded by Marea Therapeutics.

Design
Phase 2b, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT (part A); N=236; 24 weeks of dosing
Population
Fasting triglycerides ≥150 mg/dL and remnant cholesterol ≥30 mg/dL; median TG 250 mg/dL; mean age 60, 39% women
Comparison
MAR001 300, 450 or 900 mg SC every 4 weeks vs placebo
Primary endpoint
Placebo-adjusted change in fasting TG at week 12 −39.1, −40.5 and −45.3 points (p<0.001 for all)
Safety
Adverse events 69% (pooled MAR001) vs 78%

JAMA · Nov 8, 2026 · PMID 43000001

Single-pill combination therapy with team-based care for uncontrolled hypertension

Bottom lineA 10 mm Hg difference sustained for 18 months is large. The trial tests a package (free single-pill combinations plus team support), so the pill and the team cannot be separated. It supports starting or switching to single-pill combinations and using nurse-led titration with home BP readings.

Design
Pragmatic, unblinded, cluster-randomized trial; 40 primary care clinics in Colombia and Jamaica; N=1,706; 18 months
Population
Adults with uncontrolled hypertension; mean age 61, 70% women; 98% already on antihypertensives
Comparison
Single-pill combinations supplied to patients and delivered through team-based care (training, health coaching, home BP monitoring, audit and feedback) vs standard care
Primary endpoint
Systolic BP change at 18 months −19.1 vs −9.0 mm Hg; difference −10.1 mm Hg (−13.3 to −6.9)
Control
BP <130/80 mm Hg in 69.5% vs 29.2%
Safety
Death or hospitalization 7.9% vs 9.9%
Circulation · Nov 10, 2026 · PMID 43006184

FRAGILE: off-pump vs on-pump CABG in prefrail and frail patients

Bottom lineNo significant difference, with every point estimate leaning toward off-pump surgery. The trial was too small to settle it. Frailty alone is not a reason to insist on either technique; surgeon experience still decides.

Design
Multicentre, open-label RCT; N=508; 30 days and 1 year
Population
Age ≥60 (median 68), prefrail or frail, undergoing CABG; 30.5% women
Comparison
Off-pump vs on-pump CABG
Primary endpoint
30-day death, MI, stroke, renal failure, ARDS or reoperation for bleeding 10.9% vs 16.0%; RR for on-pump 1.47 (0.94–2.31; p=0.09)
Secondary
Wound infection 3.5% vs 8.0% (p=0.04); 1-year survival 96.1% vs 92.8% (p=0.099); no difference in neurocognitive or quality-of-life outcomes

Circulation · Nov 8, 2026 · PMID 42999953

DAPARHT: dapagliflozin for kidney protection after heart transplantation

Bottom lineThe first adequately sized RCT of an SGLT2 inhibitor after heart transplant. Dapagliflozin was safe under immunosuppression and modestly improved the eGFR slope. This is a surrogate over one year with a confidence interval that nearly touches zero, and there are no data on dialysis, rejection or vasculopathy. Reasonable to use in recipients with CKD or diabetes.

Design
Double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT at 6 transplant centres in Northern Europe; N=430; 12 months
Population
Adult heart transplant recipients at least 1 year after transplant (median 7 years), eGFR ≥25 (mean 67); mean age 53, 29% women
Comparison
Dapagliflozin 10 mg daily vs placebo
Primary endpoint
eGFR change from week 2 to month 12 +2.0 vs +0.3 mL/min/1.73 m²; difference 1.7 (0.1–3.3; p=0.03)
Safety
No unexpected signals; adverse events and infections similar

Europace · Nov 13, 2026 · PMID 43023359

BOX-AF: modified posterior wall ablation added to PVI in persistent AF

Bottom lineAnother neutral trial of empirical posterior wall ablation, consistent with CAPLA. With COAST-AF this week, the message for a first persistent-AF ablation is unchanged: durable PVI, and no routine extra lesions.

Design
Multicentre, single-blind RCT; N=276 (269 analysed); 12 months
Population
Persistent AF, first radiofrequency ablation
Comparison
PVI plus modified posterior wall ablation vs PVI alone
Primary endpoint
Freedom from atrial arrhythmia >30 s off antiarrhythmic drugs at 12 months 86.3% vs 86.9%, HR 1.05 (0.54–2.01; p=0.89)
Safety
Adverse events similar; longer procedure and ablation times. Acute posterior wall isolation was achieved in only 65.5%

NEJM · Nov 12, 2026 · PMID 43013818

PEPPER: aspirin vs warfarin vs rivaroxaban after hip or knee replacement

Bottom lineAspirin could not be shown to be as effective as anticoagulants, and anticoagulants could not be shown to be as safe as aspirin. Absolute differences are below 1 point in both directions. For cardiology consults: patients already on a DOAC or with prior VTE should not be stepped down to aspirin, and aspirin-only prophylaxis remains defensible for low-risk patients. Funded by PCORI.

Design
Comparative-effectiveness, non-inferiority RCT with blinded outcome assessment; N=18,883 randomized, 15,317 per protocol; 6 months
Population
Elective total hip or knee arthroplasty
Comparison
Aspirin 81 mg twice daily vs warfarin (INR target 2.0) vs rivaroxaban 10 mg daily, each for 30 days
Primary endpoint
Death or symptomatic VTE: aspirin vs rivaroxaban 1.24% vs 0.94% (difference 0.30 points, −0.20 to 0.81); aspirin vs warfarin 1.24% vs 1.01% (0.23, −0.34 to 0.81). Non-inferiority not shown against a 0.69-point margin
Safety
Bleeding, wound reoperation or MI: rivaroxaban 3.70% vs aspirin 3.02% (0.68 points, −0.04 to 1.41); warfarin 3.65% vs aspirin 2.88% (0.77, −0.04 to 1.58)
BMJ · Nov 11, 2026 · PMID 43014822

Withholding ACE inhibitors or ARBs on the morning of non-cardiac surgery

Bottom lineWithholding the morning dose prevents intraoperative hypotension but, as in the Stop-or-Not trial, clinical outcomes did not change. Either approach is acceptable; withholding is the simpler choice when hypotension is a particular concern (severe aortic stenosis, frailty).

Design
Multicentre, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT at 5 hospitals in China; N=2,036 (2,012 analysed)
Population
Age 60–80 on an ACE inhibitor or ARB, non-cardiac surgery under general anaesthesia
Comparison
Placebo (drug withheld) vs usual ACE inhibitor or ARB on the morning of surgery
Primary endpoint
Perioperative hypotension needing treatment 31.2% vs 45.5%; adjusted OR 0.52 (0.43–0.63); −14.4 points, NNT about 7
Safety
Vasopressor use 28.5% vs 41.1%; no difference in postoperative hypotension, 30-day complications or death
