A quiet week for practice-changing cardiology.

No phase 3 cardiology trial in the top-tier journals met the practice-changing bar this week. HFSA 2026 is still running, and none of its late-breakers so far has a simultaneous peer-reviewed publication. This issue is short on purpose.

Key takeaways

  • ACC pathway · Oct 5
    Resistant hypertension, step by step. Confirm with ABPM and check adherence and interfering drugs. Optimize chlorthalidone or indapamide in a single-pill combination, then add an MRA as the fourth drug, before newer agents or renal denervation.
  • Meta-analysis
    Intensive BP lowering works just as well in CKD. Across 27 RCTs (n=88,830), each 10 mm Hg lower SBP cut CV events by 15%, with a larger absolute benefit in CKD (4.3 vs 2.7 fewer per 1,000 person-years). eGFR dips rose, but kidney failure did not.
  • Trial
    CONDUCT-AF: after AV-node ablation, left bundle branch area pacing matched biventricular pacing for LVEF recovery (both reached 46%), with shorter procedures. Small trial with a surrogate endpoint, but it supports CSP first.
  • FDA · Oct 5
    CardioMEMS update: patients can see their own PA pressures in an app, and clinicians can set a Dynamic Treatment Plan that sends medication-adjustment instructions. Rollout is expected later this year.
02 · Guidelines & statements

Optimizing Blood Pressure Care in Patients With Resistant Hypertension: 2026 ACC Expert Consensus Decision Pathway

JACC · Oct 5 · PMID 42831847doi:10.1016/j.jacc.2026.08.028

Puts the 2025 AHA/ACC guideline into practice. It does not replace it and has no new graded recommendations. Resistant HTN means BP ≥130/80 mm Hg despite maximally tolerated doses of ≥3 complementary classes including a diuretic, or BP controlled only on ≥4 classes.

The sequence: rule out pseudoresistance (technique, ABPM preferred, adherence, interfering drugs and supplements, secondary causes including primary aldosteronism), then optimize the thiazide-like diuretic in a single-pill combination, then add spironolactone or eplerenone, then consider newer agents, renal denervation or specialist referral.

Editorial note. The full text was not accessible. This summary draws on the abstract record and the ACC summary, so specific renal-denervation selection criteria were not verified.

By Topic

05 · Coronary & prevention

Coronary & prevention

JAMA Cardiology · Oct 7, 2026 · PMID 42842238

Intensive vs standard BP lowering with and without CKD: meta-analysis of 27 RCTs

Bottom lineCKD is not a reason to accept a higher BP target: the relative CV benefit is the same and the absolute benefit is larger. Expect and tolerate early eGFR dips, because they did not lead to kidney failure. Trial-level data, standardized per 10 mm Hg.

Design
Systematic review and meta-analysis of 27 RCTs with ≥1 year follow-up; N=88,830
Population
Adults with and without CKD
Comparison
Intensive vs standard BP lowering, standardized per 10 mm Hg of achieved SBP difference
Primary endpoint
Major CV events: RR 0.85 (0.81–0.89) per 10 mm Hg, no heterogeneity by CKD. Absolute reduction 4.3 (CKD) vs 2.7 (no CKD) per 1,000 person-years.
Safety
Kidney failure RR 0.97 (0.84–1.11). ≥30% eGFR decline RR 1.42 (1.28–1.58); ≥40% decline RR 1.47 (1.30–1.66).
06 · Heart failure & cardiomyopathy

Heart failure & cardiomyopathy

JACC: Heart Failure · Oct 9, 2026 · PMID 42853165

LINK-HF2: AI-enabled wearable monitoring after HF hospitalization

Bottom lineThe AI detected deterioration, but clinician responses did not reduce HF events. Detection is not enough without a clear treatment response. Not a reason to adopt wearable AI monitoring yet; small feasibility trial.

Design
Randomized, open-label, blinded-endpoint feasibility trial; 5 VA centers; N=171; up to 90 days
Population
Adults discharged after acute HF hospitalization
Comparison
Multisensor patch with personalized AI alerts and a clinician response algorithm vs patch without alerts
Primary endpoint
Feasibility met (72% adherence; median response 1 day). HF rehospitalization 16.3% vs 18.7%; HR 0.90 (0.43–1.84). Composite of rehospitalization, ED visit or death: HR 1.03 (0.63–1.70).
Safety
No safety signal reported
07 · Electrophysiology

Electrophysiology

European Heart Journal · Oct 6, 2026 · PMID 42834810

CONDUCT-AF: conduction system vs biventricular pacing after AV-node ablation

Bottom lineIn "pace-and-ablate" HF patients, left bundle branch area pacing gives the same LVEF recovery as BiV with a simpler, shorter procedure. That supports CSP first where operators are experienced. Small investigator-initiated trial with a surrogate endpoint; clinical outcome data are still needed.

Design
Multicenter RCT, 10 European centers; non-inferiority; N=82; 6 months; core-lab echo
Population
Refractory AF, HF with LVEF <50%, QRS ≤120 ms, NT-proBNP >600 ng/L; mean age 72
Comparison
Left bundle branch area pacing vs biventricular pacing
Primary endpoint
LVEF change: CSP 36% → 46%, BVP 34% → 46%; difference 0.4 points (−3.1 to 3.8); non-inferiority met (P=.002)
Safety
Similar worsening HF or CV death. Paced QRS 14 ms narrower; shorter procedure and fluoroscopy times with CSP.

09 · Imaging, vascular & other

Imaging, vascular & other

JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · Oct 5, 2026 · PMID 42836810

STORM-PE: vacuum thrombectomy vs anticoagulation alone in intermediate-high-risk PE, 90-day function

Bottom lineA signal for better functional recovery, but these are secondary endpoints in a 100-patient trial, and only about two-thirds of patients completed the walk test. It does not settle routine thrombectomy; wait for the larger outcome trials.

Design
RCT, 22 international sites; N=100. The primary endpoint was RV/LV ratio at 48 h; this paper reports the 90-day secondary endpoints.
Population
Acute intermediate-high-risk PE
Comparison
Computer-assisted vacuum thrombectomy + anticoagulation vs anticoagulation alone
Main findings
6-min walk 479 vs 368 m (P=.003); NYHA I 97% vs 76%. Dyspnea and QoL scores similar.
Safety
No differences in all-cause or PE-related mortality or symptomatic recurrence at 90 days
