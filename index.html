A quiet week for practice-changing cardiology.

No phase 3 cardiology trial in the top-tier journals met the practice-changing bar this week, and no new FDA cardiovascular approval was found for Oct 12–18. The useful items are a meta-analysis on the timing of complete revascularization in STEMI, the bleeding profile of asundexian, a UK trial of early discharge on subcutaneous furosemide, and 2-year safety data that argue against interatrial shunts in HFpEF. This issue was published late, on Oct 23.

Key takeaways

  • Meta-analysis
    STEMI with multivessel disease: stage the non-culprit PCI. Across 4 RCTs (n=2,651), immediate complete revascularization had higher 1-year mortality than staged (HR 1.50, 95% CI 1.02–2.21). The only MI benefit came from procedural MIs. Few deaths and a fragile signal, but it does not support doing everything at the index procedure.
  • Trial
    OCEANIC-STROKE bleeding: adding asundexian to antiplatelet therapy did not increase major bleeding (1.9% vs 1.7%) or intracranial haemorrhage (0.7% vs 0.6%), but lower GI bleeding rose (1.7% vs 1.0%). It is the first large dataset to support factor XIa inhibition on top of antiplatelets.
  • Trial
    SUBCUT-HF II: discharging HF patients early on a wearable subcutaneous furosemide pump gave 4.0 more days alive and out of hospital at 30 days and shortened the index stay by 5.5 days. HF readmissions were higher (14% vs 6%). Small and open-label.
  • Trial
    RELIEVE-HF at 2 years: the interatrial shunt stayed patent and caused no device-related major events, but in HFpEF it was linked to more major adverse events, bleeding and kidney decline. Don't refer HFpEF patients for shunts outside trials.

05 · Coronary & prevention

Coronary & prevention

JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · Oct 12, 2026 · PMID 42863871

Immediate vs staged complete revascularization in STEMI with multivessel disease: meta-analysis of RCTs

Bottom lineKeep complete revascularization in STEMI, but stage the non-culprit lesions rather than doing them at the index procedure in most patients. The mortality signal rests on few deaths (the prediction interval crosses 1), and the MI reduction with immediate PCI came only from procedural MIs.

Design
Pairwise random-effects meta-analysis of 4 multicenter RCTs with second-generation DES and ≥1 year follow-up; N=2,651
Population
STEMI with multivessel disease
Comparison
Immediate (index-procedure) vs staged complete revascularization
Primary endpoint
All-cause death at 1 year: HR 1.50 (1.02–2.21); prediction interval 0.80–2.81; I² 0%
Secondary
MI by protocol definitions (including procedural) HR 0.58 (0.36–0.94); spontaneous MI HR 0.91 (0.55–1.48). Cardiac death and unplanned revascularization similar.
Circulation · Oct 13, 2026 · PMID 42866879

OCEANIC-STROKE: bleeding with asundexian vs placebo on top of antiplatelet therapy

Bottom lineFactor XIa inhibition added to single or dual antiplatelet therapy did not raise major or intracranial bleeding. Lower GI (mainly rectal) bleeding was the one clear signal. This is a stroke population; it does not yet tell us how asundexian behaves with DAPT after ACS or PCI. Sponsor-funded (Bayer).

Design
Prespecified bleeding analysis of a phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT; safety population N=12,254
Population
Noncardioembolic ischaemic stroke or high-risk TIA, on antiplatelet therapy (including planned DAPT)
Comparison
Asundexian (n=6,124) vs placebo (n=6,130)
Bleeding
ISTH major 1.9% vs 1.7%; csHR 1.10 (0.85–1.44). Major or CRNM 5.5% vs 5.0%; csHR 1.12 (0.96–1.30). Any bleeding 12.5% vs 12.3%.
Safety signals
Symptomatic ICH 0.7% vs 0.6%. Any GI bleeding 3.0% vs 2.4% (csHR 1.28, 1.03–1.59); lower GI 1.7% vs 1.0% (csHR 1.75, 1.27–2.41).
06 · Heart failure & cardiomyopathy

Heart failure & cardiomyopathy

JACC · Oct 13, 2026 · PMID 42872757

SUBCUT-HF II: early discharge on a subcutaneous furosemide minipump

Bottom lineEarly discharge on a wearable subcutaneous furosemide pump, with a review every 3 days, freed up about 4 days in 30 and cut the index stay by 5.5 days. HF readmissions were more than double (not significant), so this needs a structured outpatient decongestion service. A small, open-label UK trial.

Design
Multicenter, open-label, randomized, active-controlled trial in the UK; N=172; 60-day follow-up
Population
Hospitalized HF patients still needing parenteral diuretics
Comparison
Early discharge on pH-neutral subcutaneous furosemide by patient-operated minipump (n=92) vs usual inpatient care (n=80)
Primary endpoint
Days alive and out of hospital at 30 days: +4.0 days (2.1–5.8). Index stay 5.5 days shorter (3.6–7.4). At 60 days, +4.2 days (0.9–7.6).
Safety
HF readmission 14% vs 6% (OR 2.45, 0.81–7.43). Serious adverse events 25% vs 23%. Device problems in 3% of pump placements, none serious.
JACC: Heart Failure · Oct 13, 2026 · PMID 42886087

RELIEVE-HF: 2-year safety of an interatrial shunt by ejection fraction

Bottom lineThe device works mechanically, but HFpEF patients did worse with it: more major adverse events, bleeding and kidney decline. Together with the neutral primary trial, this argues against interatrial shunting in HFpEF. Any future role is in HFrEF, which needs its own trial. Device-company-funded.

Design
Prespecified LVEF-stratified safety analysis of a sham-controlled RCT; 605 enrolled (508 randomized, 97 roll-in); 2 years
Population
Symptomatic HF; stratified into HFrEF (LVEF ≤40%) and HFpEF (LVEF >40%)
Comparison
V-Wave interatrial shunt vs sham procedure
Device
Implant success 99.7% (369/370). No device- or procedure-related major adverse cardiovascular or neurological events (0%, 0–0.9%). Patency 99.4% at 2 years.
By phenotype
HFrEF: no excess major events, bleeding or renal decline, with fewer serious adverse events. HFpEF: more major events, serious adverse events, major bleeding and renal decline with the shunt.
European Journal of Heart Failure · Oct 17, 2026 · PMID 42888496

NT-proBNP thresholds for acute HF in breathless ED patients: meta-analysis

Bottom lineUse ≤300 ng/L to rule acute HF out and age-adjusted cut-offs (450 / 900 / 1,800 ng/L) to rule it in. The age-adjusted thresholds held up in obesity but were less accurate in CKD.

Design
Systematic review and meta-analysis of 31 diagnostic studies; N=26,948
Population
Dyspneic patients in the emergency department
Rule-out
≤300 ng/L: sensitivity 97.9%, NPV 97.0%, specificity 46.2%
Rule-in
Age-dependent (≥450 if <50 y, ≥900 if 50–75 y, ≥1,800 if ≥75 y; n=14,410): AUC 0.84, sensitivity 85.9%, specificity 82.0%
Subgroups
Obesity (BMI ≥30): AUC 0.82. CKD: AUC 0.77, specificity 62.6%.

09 · Imaging, vascular & other

Imaging, vascular & other

JACC: Cardiovascular Imaging · Oct 15, 2026 · PMID 42886154

CAUGHT-CAD: statins shift coronary calcium toward higher density on serial CCTA

Bottom lineOn serial CCTA, statins shrank non-calcified plaque and pushed calcium toward higher density, most of all with ≥30% LDL-C lowering. A rising calcium score on a statin can mean plaque stabilization rather than treatment failure, so read the composition, not just the Agatston number. Post hoc, imaging endpoints only.

Design
Post hoc analysis of an RCT with coronary CTA at baseline and 3 years; 840 lesions in 338 participants
Population
Asymptomatic, statin-naive, intermediate-risk adults; mean age 58
Comparison
CAC-guided primary prevention strategy including statins (48% treated) vs usual care
Primary endpoint
Per-lesion change in calcified plaque subtypes. Statins: more intermediate-density (β 0.89, p=0.02) and high-density calcium (β 0.79, p=0.001), and regression of non-calcified plaque (β −9.83, p<0.001).
Dose-response
LDL-C reduction ≥30% linked to more high-density (β 1.11) and very-high-density calcium (β 1.68)
