A quiet week for practice-changing cardiology.

No cardiology phase 3 trial in the top-tier journals met the practice-changing bar this week. Most of the large trials published were obesity and diabetes studies released during EASD 2026. This issue is short on purpose.

Key takeaways

  • FDA · Sept 30
    Mavacamten is now approved for adolescents (12 to <18 years, ≥30 kg) with symptomatic obstructive HCM, based on SCOUT-HCM. It is the first approved drug for adolescent oHCM, so refer eligible patients to an HCM center.
  • Class I recall
    Abiomed automated Impella controller: a purge-related component defect, with 37 serious injuries and 3 deaths reported. Devices stay in use while components are replaced. Shock teams should review the field-safety notice.
  • Trial
    SWEDEGRAFT at 7 years: no-touch vein harvesting did not reduce death, MI or repeat revascularization (19.8% vs 20.4%) and causes more leg-wound complications. Routine use is not supported.
  • Trial
    ACHIEVE-4: oral orforglipron was non-inferior to insulin glargine for MACE-4 (4.2% vs 5.0%; HR 0.84). This shows safety, not benefit. GLP-1 RAs and tirzepatide with outcome data remain first-line in T2D with ASCVD.
02 · Guidelines & statements

2026 AHA/ACC/ACS/ASNC/HRS/SCA/SCCT/SCMR/SVM Guideline for Perioperative Cardiovascular Management for Noncardiac Surgery

JACC 88(13):1543–1643 · Sept 28–29doi:10.1016/j.jacc.2026.06.017Circulation version

Editorial caveat. The abstract describes the same evidence base as the 2024 perioperative guideline (literature search Aug 2022 to Mar 2023) and says it supersedes the 2014 guideline. It does not mention 2024. The full text was not accessible, so new or changed Class I/III recommendations could not be confirmed.

The core framework is unchanged: stepwise risk assessment; stress testing only when indicated regardless of surgery; CCTA as an option (2b) when functional capacity is poor or unknown; selective NT-proBNP and troponin; stop SGLT2i 3–4 days before surgery; delay elective surgery ≥12 months after DES for ACS (Class 1) and ≥6 months for CCS (2a); bridge anticoagulation only at the highest thrombotic risk; follow up new postoperative AF closely.

EJHF Expert Consensus: preventing HF across the cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic spectrum

Eur J Heart Fail · Oct 1doi:10.1093/ejhf/xuag301

Recommends starting RASi, SGLT2i, finerenone and incretin-based agents early and in parallel in at-risk and pre-HF patients with T2D, CKD or obesity, with HFpEF prevention as the main target. No new graded recommendations.

AHA Scientific Statement: undertreated antithrombotic therapy

Circulation · Oct 1doi:10.1161/CIR.0000000000001474

Covers omission, underdosing and nonadherence in AF, VTE and PAD and their link to stroke, recurrent thrombosis and death, and supports antithrombotic stewardship. Off-label DOAC underdosing in AF is the target most relevant to clinics.

AF Ablation Academic Research Consortium (AFA-ARC), HRS-endorsed

Circ Arrhythm Electrophysiol · Sept 29doi:10.1161/CIRCEP.126.015082

Standard effectiveness and safety endpoints and trial designs for pivotal AF-ablation device trials, including PFA, developed with FDA input. It will shape how future ablation trials are read. It is not a clinical guideline.

2026 ACC Scientific Statement: nutrition for preventing and managing CVD

JACC · Sept 30doi:10.1016/j.jacc.2026.08.025

A statement on dietary patterns, useful for patient counselling. No graded recommendations.

By Topic

05 · Coronary & prevention

Coronary & prevention

JAMA Cardiology · Sept 30, 2026 · PMID 42814420

SWEDEGRAFT: no-touch vs conventional saphenous vein harvesting in CABG, 7 years

Bottom lineRoutine no-touch harvesting is not supported: no clinical benefit at 7 years and more leg morbidity. The CI is wide, so the trial had limited power for a modest effect. Academic funding.

Design
Multicenter RCT (8 Swedish centers and 1 Danish), registry follow-up; mITT N=900; median 6.5 years
Population
Primary, nonemergent, isolated CABG; mean age 67; 12% women; 47% nonelective
Comparison
No-touch harvesting (n=454) vs conventional (n=446)
Primary endpoint
Death, MI or repeat revascularization: 19.8% vs 20.4%; HR 0.98 (0.73–1.31); adjusted 7-year risk difference −0.8% (−6.6 to 4.6). All components neutral.
Safety
More leg-wound complications with no-touch, as previously reported
Lancet · Sept 30, 2026 · PMID 42815506

ACHIEVE-4: orforglipron vs insulin glargine for cardiovascular safety in T2D

Bottom lineRules out excess CV harm for the first oral non-peptide GLP-1 RA, but does not show benefit: the comparator was insulin, the trial was open-label, and there were only 124 events. In T2D with ASCVD, keep agents with proven MACE reduction first. Funded by Eli Lilly.

Design
Phase 3, event-driven, open-label, non-inferiority; N=2,749; 317 sites; median 2 years
Population
T2D (HbA1c 7.0–10.5%), BMI ≥25, all with established CVD (86%) or CKD (38%); mean age 63
Comparison
Oral orforglipron daily (up to 36 mg) vs titrated insulin glargine
Primary endpoint
MACE-4: 4.2% vs 5.0%; HR 0.84 (0.59–1.20); non-inferiority met; absolute difference −0.8% (not significant for superiority)
Safety
GI adverse events 62.1% vs 14.2%; significant hypoglycaemia 6.8% vs 19.2%; deaths 1.4% vs 3.2%
NEJM · Sept 29 · PMID 42814954  |  Lancet · Sept 29 · PMID 42810372

TRIUMPH-1 & TRIUMPH-2: retatrutide for obesity, with and without T2D

Bottom lineThe largest drug-induced weight loss yet seen in phase 3, but the endpoints are weight only and there are no CV outcome data. Watch for hypotension in patients on several antihypertensives. Not yet approved. Funded by Eli Lilly.

Design
Phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 80 weeks. TRIUMPH-1 N=2,339; TRIUMPH-2 N=1,152
Population
TRIUMPH-1: obesity without diabetes. TRIUMPH-2: BMI ≥27 with T2D (mean BMI 38.2)
Comparison
Retatrutide 4, 9 or 12 mg weekly vs placebo
Primary endpoint
TRIUMPH-1: −23.7% / −25.0% vs −3.9% (9 / 12 mg). TRIUMPH-2: −16.8% / −18.8% vs −5.1%; differences −11.8% and −13.8%
Safety
Mainly GI. Hypotension 5–6% vs <1% and dysaesthesia 6–7% vs 1% at higher doses; stopped for adverse events 8–12% vs 5%
NEJM · Oct 1, 2026 · PMID 42820639

SYNCHRONIZE-2: survodutide in obesity with T2D

Bottom lineA modest effect in T2D compared with tirzepatide- or retatrutide-class agents, with a heavy GI burden. No cardiology relevance until outcome data arrive. Funded by Boehringer Ingelheim.

Design
Phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled; N=752; 76 weeks
Population
T2D with BMI ≥27 (mean 36.5; HbA1c 7.4%)
Comparison
Survodutide 3.6 or 6.0 mg weekly vs placebo
Primary endpoint
Weight −8.2% / −9.8% vs −3.9%; ≥5% loss in 57.6% / 64.5% vs 35.1%
Safety
GI adverse events 73–78% vs 39%

07 · Imaging, vascular & other

Imaging, vascular & other

J Cardiovasc Comput Tomogr · Sept 30, 2026 · PMID 42816231

HALT after TAVR: systematic review and network meta-analysis

Bottom lineSupports the current approach: no routine CT screening for HALT, and no OAC for HALT alone in asymptomatic patients with normal gradients. The effect on durability is unknown. Data are mostly observational, with mixed CT protocols and timing.

Design
Pairwise and network meta-analysis of 59 mostly observational studies; N=16,269
Population
Post-TAVR patients with CT follow-up
Comparison
By valve platform, antithrombotic regimen and patient factors
Main findings
HALT in 11.0%. Lower with self-expanding supra-annular valves and higher with intra-annular and Lotus valves, compared with balloon-expandable. Less with OAC than without; DOAC and warfarin similar. Small rise in gradient; no short-term clinical events.
