A quiet week for practice-changing cardiology.

No phase 3 cardiology trial in the top-tier journals met the practice-changing bar for Oct 19–25, and no congress ran. The useful items are a large pragmatic trial of point-of-care troponin in the ED, an OPTION-STEMI analysis on immediate revascularization in older patients, a meta-analysis of PCI before TAVI, a sham-controlled meta-analysis of AF ablation, and FDA approval of PASCAL for tricuspid repair. Published Monday, Oct 26.

Key takeaways

  • Trial
    Point-of-care hs-troponin in the ED (ICare-FASTER). In a stepped-wedge cluster trial of 59,980 presentations, a point-of-care hs-cTnI assay cut ED stay by 47 minutes (13%), with the same 30-day MI or cardiac death rate (0.39% vs 0.39%).
  • Trial
    Older STEMI patients: stage the non-culprit PCI. In OPTION-STEMI, patients ≥70 had more death, MI or repeat revascularization at 1 year with immediate complete revascularization than with staged in-hospital PCI (22.3% vs 13.8%; HR 1.68). No difference under 70. Post hoc.
  • Meta-analysis
    CAD before TAVI. Across 4 RCTs (n=1,476), routine PCI lowered MACE (RR 0.80, 0.65–0.98) but raised major bleeding (RR 1.57, 1.07–2.32).
  • Meta-analysis
    AF ablation beats sham for symptoms. Pooling 3 sham-controlled trials (n=448), ablation improved AFEQT by 13.5 points (95% CrI 1.6–26.9) and halved AF recurrence (RR 0.49).
  • FDA
    PASCAL Precision approved for tricuspid TEER (Oct 21) in symptomatic severe TR at intermediate or higher surgical risk, based on CLASP II TR. One-year data come at TCT next week.
Guidelines & statements

Management of Heart Transplant Recipients for the Non-Transplant Health Care Provider: HFA/ACNAP Clinical Consensus Statement

Eur J Heart Fail · Oct 19 · PMID 42895118doi:10.1093/ejhf/xuag326

A practical guide for cardiologists and GPs who see transplant recipients outside transplant centres: care by time since transplant, immunosuppressant monitoring, recognizing rejection, infection prophylaxis, and surveillance for allograft vasculopathy, cancer, CKD and metabolic disease. Also covers pregnancy, non-cardiac surgery and palliative care, and lists when to refer back early. No graded recommendations.

Multidisciplinary Collaboration in the Performance of Aortic Surgery: AHA Scientific Statement

Circulation · Oct 22 · PMID 42910416doi:10.1161/CIR.0000000000001490

Surgeon, anaesthesiologist and perfusionist views on aortic surgery: single-lung ventilation, cooling and rewarming, and cerebral, myocardial and visceral protection. A team-process document with no graded recommendations.

Coronary & prevention

Circulation · Oct 19, 2026 · PMID 42895015

ICare-FASTER: point-of-care high-sensitivity troponin in the emergency department

Bottom lineA point-of-care hs-cTnI assay inside an established single-sample rule-out pathway saves about 45 minutes per chest-pain visit with no loss of safety. The gain is in throughput, not outcomes, and it depends on a validated assay and pathway.

Design
Pragmatic stepped-wedge cluster-randomized trial at 6 New Zealand sites, Feb 2023 – Jan 2025; 59,980 ED presentations (44,747 people)
Population
Adults with possible MI in the ED; mean age 61, 49.5% female
Comparison
Accelerated diagnostic pathway with whole-blood point-of-care hs-cTnI vs the same pathway with central-lab hs-cTn
Primary endpoint
ED length of stay −13% (9–16%), or −47 minutes (33–61) from a mean of 376 minutes
Safety
30-day MI or cardiac death 0.39% vs 0.39%
JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions · Oct 22, 2026 · PMID 42925297

OPTION-STEMI: immediate vs staged complete revascularization by age

Bottom lineIn patients 70 or older, stage the non-culprit PCI during the same admission rather than doing it at the index procedure. This is a post hoc subgroup, so it generates a hypothesis, but it agrees with last week's meta-analysis favouring staged PCI. Funded partly by Boston Scientific.

Design
Post hoc age analysis of a randomized trial; N=994; 1 year
Population
STEMI with multivessel disease
Comparison
Immediate (index-procedure) vs in-hospital staged complete revascularization
Primary endpoint
Death, MI or unplanned revascularization. Age ≥70: 22.3% vs 13.8%, HR 1.68 (1.06–2.69). Age <70: 6.8% vs 8.4%, HR 0.80 (0.45–1.45).
Interaction
p=0.015 with age as a continuous variable; risk with immediate CR rose from about age 67

JAMA · Oct 24, 2026 · PMID 42925557

SGLT2 inhibitors and haemoglobin, iron and erythropoiesis: meta-analysis of 85 RCTs

Bottom lineExpect about +0.6 g/dL haemoglobin and +2–3% haematocrit after starting an SGLT2 inhibitor. EPO rises too, so this reflects erythropoiesis rather than just haemoconcentration, and a modest rise on treatment should not prompt an erythrocytosis work-up. Transferrin saturation falls slightly, which matters when reading iron studies in HF.

Design
Systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of 85 double-blind RCTs; N=70,543; median 24 weeks
Population
Cardiovascular disease, diabetes, heart failure or CKD
Comparison
SGLT2 inhibitor vs placebo (78 trials) or active comparator (7)
Primary endpoint
Haemoglobin +0.60 g/dL (0.55–0.66); haematocrit +2.36% (2.19–2.52)
Secondary
EPO +2.11 mIU/mL (0.50–3.71); transferrin saturation −0.84% (−1.21 to −0.47)

Heart Rhythm · Oct 22, 2026 · PMID 42913986

Catheter ablation vs sham for symptomatic AF: Bayesian meta-analysis of randomized trials

Bottom lineThe quality-of-life gain from AF ablation is not just placebo, though its size is uncertain: heterogeneity was high, mostly from PVI-SHAM-AF. This supports offering ablation for symptom relief. Certainty is low for quality of life and moderate for recurrence.

Design
Systematic review and Bayesian random-effects meta-analysis; 4 sham-controlled RCTs (n=468), 3 pooled (n=448)
Population
Symptomatic atrial fibrillation
Comparison
Catheter ablation vs sham procedure
Primary endpoint
AFEQT +13.5 points (95% CrI 1.6–26.9); probability of exceeding the 5-point minimal important difference 0.94. Without PVI-SHAM-AF: +18.9 (6.7–31.4).
Recurrence
AF recurrence RR 0.49 (0.17–0.89), lower with ablation in every trial; I² 85% for quality of life
Heart Rhythm · Oct 23, 2026 · PMID 42909425

Q-INDEX: very high-power short-duration vs ablation-index-guided vs hybrid PVI

Bottom lineUniform 90 W/4 s ablation more than halves ablation time but may cost durability; a hybrid approach, with ablation-index lesions on the thick anterior wall, keeps most of the speed. The trial was powered only for ablation time, so the efficacy differences are exploratory.

Design
Multicenter, open-label RCT, 1:1:1; N=150; Korea; 1 year
Population
Paroxysmal AF, first PVI with the QDOT MICRO catheter
Comparison
Ablation-index-guided vs hybrid vs uniform 90 W/4 s
Primary endpoint
Total ablation time 2,220 vs 1,450 vs 794 s (p<0.001)
Efficacy & safety
1-year freedom from atrial arrhythmia 92.0% vs 85.4% vs 77.6% (index-guided vs 90 W, p=0.049). No tamponade; steam pops only at 90 W; 2 minor strokes, both in the 90 W arm.

EuroIntervention · Oct 19, 2026 · PMID 42910974

Coronary artery disease management before TAVI: meta-analysis of randomized trials

Bottom lineRoutine PCI of significant CAD around TAVI reduces ischaemic events at the cost of more major bleeding; physiology-guided PCI ranked best in the network analysis. Weigh the patient's bleeding risk. Only 4 trials contribute to the pooled estimate.

Design
Pairwise and network meta-analysis; 5 RCTs (N=1,644), 4 in the primary synthesis (N=1,476)
Population
Severe aortic stenosis undergoing TAVI with significant CAD
Comparison
Routine PCI (angiography- or physiology-guided) vs conservative management
Primary endpoint
MACE RR 0.80 (0.65–0.98); unplanned revascularization RR 0.30 (0.11–0.83)
Safety
Major bleeding RR 1.57 (1.07–2.32)
