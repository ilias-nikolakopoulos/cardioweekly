Key takeaways

TCT 2026 ran from Oct 31 to Nov 3, across the end of the window, so Congress Watch includes papers published through Nov 3. Three trials meet the practice-changing bar. Published Thursday, Nov 5.

  • Practice-changing
    TAVR for moderate AS with at-risk features (PROGRESS). TAVR cut death or heart-failure events at 2 years compared with surveillance (16.7% vs 23.9%; −7.2 points, NNT about 14). The benefit came from HF events (10.8% vs 19.0%), not mortality (8.4% vs 7.3%). Open-label and Edwards-funded.
  • Practice-changing
    After LAAO, aspirin alone is enough (SIMPLAAFY). In 1,857 patients after uncomplicated LAAC, aspirin alone and reduced-dose NOAC were both non-inferior to 6 months of DAPT. Aspirin alone had the fewest events (3.3% vs 6.9%; RR 0.49) and the least major bleeding (1.7% vs 4.5%).
  • Practice-changing
    LBBAP over RV pacing for AV block with preserved EF (LEAP-Block). With expected pacing above 40%, LBBAP lowered death, HF events or biventricular upgrade at 24 months (2.4% vs 12.5%; HR 0.37, 0.19–0.73). Most of the effect was fewer HF events.
  • Trial
    CCTA can replace IVUS for PCI planning (P4). In 1,105 patients with FFR-CT ≤0.80, CCTA-guided PCI was non-inferior to IVUS-guided PCI for 12-month target-vessel failure (4.1% vs 4.6%). The margin was generous, and the trial was funded by GE HealthCare and Heartflow.
  • Meta-analysis
    0/1 h vs 0/3 h troponin. Across 110,933 presentations, the 0/1 h pathway was as safe (death or type 1 MI 0.8% vs 1.0%) but did not shorten ED stays on average (314 vs 308 minutes).
  • FDA
    Amplatzer Paraclose PVL Occluder approved (Oct 28): the first US transcatheter device designed specifically to close paravalvular leaks around surgical valves.

NEJM · Nov 2, 2026 · PMID 42970708Practice-changing

PROGRESS: TAVR vs clinical surveillance in moderate aortic stenosis

Bottom lineFor moderate AS with symptoms or early LV damage, early TAVR prevents heart-failure events. It did not reduce mortality, and the HF endpoint includes events without hospitalization in an open-label trial. Refer symptomatic moderate AS, or moderate AS with LV damage, to the heart team rather than defaulting to surveillance; in younger patients, valve durability still weighs against early intervention. Funded by Edwards.

Design
International, open-label RCT, 1:1; N=744; 2 years
Population
Moderate AS (mean AVA 1.2 cm², peak velocity 3.3 m/s) with at least one at-risk feature; mean age 78, 39% women, 46% low surgical risk
Comparison
Transfemoral TAVR with a balloon-expandable valve vs clinical surveillance
Primary endpoint
Death or HF event at 2 years 16.7% vs 23.9%; risk difference −7.2 points (−13.1 to −1.3), p=0.02; NNT about 14
Components
HF events 10.8% vs 19.0%; all-cause death 8.4% vs 7.3%
Safety
Procedure-related adverse events similar to surveillance patients who later had valve replacement
JAMA · Nov 2, 2026 · PMID 42970643Practice-changing

SIMPLAAFY: antithrombotic therapy after left atrial appendage occlusion

Bottom lineAfter an uncomplicated LAAC, aspirin alone is a reasonable default; DAPT adds bleeding without benefit. Consider reduced-dose NOAC when device-related thrombus risk is high, since it had the lowest DRT rate. Open-label, and secondary comparisons were not adjusted for multiplicity.

Design
Open-label, 3-arm, non-inferiority RCT at 87 US sites; N=1,857; 6 months
Population
Non-valvular AF after successful LAAC; mean age 76, CHA₂DS₂-VASc 4.2
Comparison
Aspirin alone for 12 months, or reduced-dose NOAC for 3 months then aspirin, vs DAPT for 6 months then aspirin
Primary endpoint
Death, stroke, systemic embolism or major bleeding: aspirin 3.3% vs 6.9% with DAPT (RR 0.49, 0.29–0.82); reduced-dose NOAC 5.1% (RR 0.75, 0.48–1.18). Both non-inferior.
Safety
Major bleeding 1.7% (aspirin), 2.7% (NOAC), 4.5% (DAPT). Device-related thrombus 2.7%, 0.9%, 3.4% (exploratory).
NEJM · Nov 1, 2026 · PMID 42969897

P4: coronary CT angiography vs intravascular ultrasound to guide PCI

Bottom lineA pre-procedure CCTA with FFR-CT can stand in for intravascular imaging for vessel sizing and planning, with event rates like IVUS. The 4.5-point margin is generous against an event rate of about 4.6%, and funding came from GE HealthCare and Heartflow. It suits centres already running a CCTA-first pathway; it is not a reason to drop IVUS for left main or complex lesions.

Design
Investigator-initiated, international, open-label non-inferiority RCT with blinded adjudication; N=1,105; 12 months
Population
Indication for revascularization, at least one lesion >70% stenosis and FFR-CT ≤0.80; median age 65, 22% women
Comparison
CCTA-guided PCI planning vs IVUS-guided PCI
Primary endpoint
Target-vessel failure 4.1% vs 4.6%; risk difference −0.42 points (−2.82 to 1.99); non-inferior (p<0.001), not superior (p=0.73)
Safety
Clinical procedural complications in 6 vs 9 patients

Lancet · Oct 29, 2026 · PMID 42956796

0/1 h vs 0/3 h troponin pathways: individual-patient meta-analysis of randomized trials

Bottom lineThe guideline-endorsed 0/1 h pathway is safe but does not shorten ED stays on average. Heterogeneity was extreme, so any gain depends on the local system. Read with last week's ICare-FASTER: throughput comes from turnaround time and discharge processes, not the retest interval alone.

Design
Systematic review and two-stage IPD meta-analysis; 5 RCTs, 110,933 presentations
Population
Suspected myocardial infarction in the ED
Comparison
0/1 h vs 0/3 h high-sensitivity troponin pathway
Primary endpoint
30-day death or new type 1 MI 0.8% vs 1.0% (OR 0.83, 0.45–1.53). ED stay 314 vs 308 min (ratio 1.03, 0.85–1.25). Direct discharge 67.0% vs 65.8%.
Caveats
Safety estimate imprecise; I² 63% for safety and 99% for ED stay; the safety outcome was refined after data receipt
Circ Cardiovasc Interv · Oct 31, 2026 · PMID 42965935

BRIGHT-4 at 1 year: bivalirudin with a high-dose post-PCI infusion vs heparin in STEMI

Bottom lineThe 30-day benefit of bivalirudin with a full-dose post-PCI infusion holds at 1 year. A reasonable choice for radial primary PCI where bivalirudin is available; the infusion is what protects against acute stent thrombosis. Single-country and open-label.

Design
Multicenter, open-label RCT in China; N=6,016; 12-month follow-up
Population
STEMI undergoing primary PCI via radial access
Comparison
Bivalirudin plus a high-dose infusion for 2–4 hours after PCI vs heparin monotherapy
Primary endpoint
Death or BARC 3–5 bleeding 4.1% vs 5.6%, HR 0.73 (0.58–0.92); −1.5 points
Secondary
BARC 3–5 bleeding 0.6% vs 1.2% (HR 0.49); stent thrombosis 0.8% vs 1.3% (HR 0.60); death 3.8% vs 4.8% (HR 0.80, 0.63–1.02)

JAMA · Oct 28, 2026 · PMID 42949742Practice-changing

LEAP-Block: left bundle-branch area pacing vs right ventricular pacing for AV block

Bottom lineIn patients who will pace a lot, LBBAP prevents heart-failure events compared with RV pacing, so it should be the first choice for high-burden pacing where operators have the skills. The effect came mainly from fewer HF events, with no difference in death, and the trial was single-country and modest-sized.

Design
Multicenter RCT at 14 Chinese hospitals; 499 randomized, 482 analysed; 24 months
Population
AV block, LVEF ≥50%, expected ventricular pacing >40%; mean age 67, 46% women
Comparison
LBBAP vs RV pacing
Primary endpoint
Death, HF event or upgrade to biventricular pacing 2.4% vs 12.5% (−10.1 points); Kaplan-Meier 5.0% vs 13.2%, HR 0.37 (0.19–0.73); NNT about 10
Secondary
HF events 3.4% vs 11.3%; no difference in death, BiV upgrade, pacing-induced cardiomyopathy or device complications
Procedure
Acute success 97.9% vs 99.6%; left bundle-branch capture confirmed in 80%

Circulation · Oct 31, 2026 · PMID 42965504

SEASON-AR: TAVR vs medical therapy for severe pure aortic regurgitation

Bottom lineThe first randomized evidence that TAVR helps patients with pure AR who cannot have surgery, driven by fewer HF admissions. It is small, open-label and used a device not available in the US; for now, it supports referral to centres with dedicated AR valves or trials rather than off-label TAVR.

Design
Investigator-initiated, open-label RCT at 18 Chinese centres; N=210; 1 year
Population
Severe pure AR, surgery high-risk or declined
Comparison
Transfemoral TAVR (VitaFlow) plus GDMT vs GDMT alone
Primary endpoint
Death, disabling stroke or HF rehospitalization 8.3% vs 24.8%, HR 0.31 (0.14–0.66); HF rehospitalization 3.7% vs 21.3%
Safety
More major or life-threatening bleeding and new pacemakers with TAVR, mostly within 30 days
JACC · Nov 3, 2026 (during TCT) · PMID 42980634

SINGLE-CLOSURE: one vs two ProGlide sutures after transfemoral TAVR

Bottom lineOne ProGlide is enough for most TAVR access, with bail-out closure devices kept at hand. Most events were minor.

Design
Open-label non-inferiority RCT at 25 Chinese centres; N=876
Population
Age ≥65 undergoing transfemoral TAVR
Comparison
Single vs double ProGlide for the main access
Primary endpoint
In-hospital VARC-3 access-related major or minor vascular complications 13.1% vs 15.1% (−2.2 points, −7.0 to 2.6); non-inferior (margin 3.19 points)
Safety
Major vascular complications 0.5% vs 1.4%; access-site stenosis 5.0% vs 7.9%; extra closure device 2.5% vs 0.9%
JAMA Cardiology · Nov 3, 2026 (during TCT) · PMID 42976368

DUOTAP: routine post-dilation of balloon-expandable TAVI valves

Bottom linePost-dilation gives a rounder, larger valve with better haemodynamics on imaging. These are surrogate endpoints from one centre, so this alone should not change practice; it may become standard if durability and clinical data follow.

Design
Single-centre (Vienna), single-blind, sham-controlled RCT; N=148; CT at 3 months
Population
Severe AS, transfemoral TAVI with a balloon-expandable valve, no high-risk CT or angiographic features
Comparison
Routine post-dilation with the delivery balloon at the same volume vs sham
Primary endpoint
Valve asymmetry index 1.2% vs 2.8% (geometric mean ratio 0.30, 0.18–0.47)
Safety
No difference in procedural adverse events; less leaflet thickening (HALT) and less mild-or-worse paravalvular leak
