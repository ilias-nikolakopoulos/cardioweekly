Key takeaways

AHA Scientific Sessions 2026 (Chicago, Nov 6–9) fell inside the window, and most of this week's papers were Nov 7 simultaneous publications. Two results meet the practice-changing bar; three large outcome trials were negative. The Sunday and Monday late-breakers were not yet published at issue time.

  • Practice-changing
    Systolic BP below 120 mm Hg after ischaemic stroke (OPTIMAL-STROKE). In 4,368 patients about a year after stroke or TIA, the intensive target cut MACE over about 4 years (10.9% vs 13.5%; HR 0.79, 0.67–0.94; NNT about 38). The price was more renal laboratory events (3.9% vs 2.2%).
  • Practice-changing
    Plozasiran for severe hypertriglyceridaemia (SHASTA-3/4). Quarterly APOC3 siRNA lowered triglycerides by about 80% and cut acute pancreatitis from 5 to 1 per 100 patient-years (rate ratio 0.22).
  • Trial
    Lp(a) lowering without benefit (Lp(a)HORIZON). In 8,350 secondary-prevention patients, pelacarsen lowered Lp(a) by 78% but MACE did not change (12.3% vs 12.1%; HR 1.02). There is still no Lp(a)-specific therapy with outcome benefit.
  • Trial
    IL-6 inhibition fails in ASCVD with CKD (ZEUS). Ziltivekimab cut hs-CRP but not MACE (17.6% vs 17.7%; HR 0.99), and serious infections rose (20.4% vs 17.9%).
  • Trial
    No systolic target below 120 mm Hg in type 2 diabetes (OPTIMAL-DIABETES). In 9,476 patients, MACE was 6.0% vs 6.7% (not significant), with more kidney-function events (5.7% vs 3.7%). Keep <130 mm Hg.
  • Trial
    Still no survival benefit from an invasive strategy in chronic coronary disease (ISCHEMIA-EXTEND). All-cause death at 11 years was 25.8% vs 26.9% (adjusted HR 0.97). The COURAGE and ISCHEMIA-CKD extensions agree.
  • Safety
    Class I recall of four lots of SAPIEN M3 Commander M delivery systems because the balloon can burst during inflation; the valve itself is not affected.

NEJM · Nov 7, 2026 · PMID pendingPractice-changing

OPTIMAL-STROKE: systolic target below 120 vs below 140 mm Hg after ischaemic stroke or TIA

Bottom lineAfter ischaemic stroke, aim for systolic BP around 120 mm Hg in patients who tolerate it, and check eGFR and electrolytes as treatment is intensified. Single-country trial; the effect in TIA alone is uncertain.

Design
Randomized trial at 30 centres in Brazil; N=4,368; median follow-up almost 4 years
Population
Previous ischaemic stroke (92%) or TIA (8%), randomized a median of 1 year after the event; mean age 64, 48% women
Comparison
Systolic target <120 vs <140 mm Hg; sustained difference about 13 mm Hg (intensive-arm mean about 122 mm Hg)
Primary endpoint
CV death, MI, stroke, or hospitalization for unstable angina or HF 10.9% vs 13.5%, HR 0.79 (0.67–0.94); −2.6 points, NNT about 38
Secondary
CV death, MI or stroke 9.8% vs 11.8% (HR 0.81, 0.68–0.98)
Safety
Renal composite 3.9% vs 2.2% (HR 1.76, 1.24–2.50; NNH about 59), mostly laboratory changes; 6 patients on long-term dialysis. TIA subgroup HR 1.34 (0.68–2.64)
NEJM · Nov 7, 2026 · PMID pending

Lp(a)HORIZON: pelacarsen in secondary prevention with high lipoprotein(a)

Bottom lineA 78% Lp(a) reduction from a baseline of about 236 nmol/L did not prevent events, so Lp(a) lowering by itself is unproven as a therapeutic target. Lp(a) remains a risk marker that should push LDL-C and other risk-factor control harder. The siRNA programmes will settle whether the problem is the drug or the hypothesis. Funded by Novartis, which has stopped the ASCVD programme.

Design
Phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT; 797 sites in 42 countries; N=8,350; median 4.7 years
Population
Prior CV event 3 months to 10 years earlier and Lp(a) ≥70 mg/dL; mean age 60, 27% women; median LDL-C about 65 mg/dL
Comparison
Pelacarsen 80 mg SC monthly vs placebo
Primary endpoint
CV death, MI, stroke or urgent coronary revascularization 12.3% vs 12.1%, HR 1.02 (0.90–1.15); neutral in Lp(a) ≥90 mg/dL (HR 1.00)
Lp(a)
64 vs 233 nmol/L at 12 months (−77.7%); LDL-C −13%
Safety
Drug-related AEs 56.8% vs 18.0%; stopped for AEs 20.6% vs 7.5%; influenza-like reactions 31.9% vs 5.8%
NEJM · Nov 7, 2026 · PMID pending

ZEUS: ziltivekimab (IL-6 inhibition) in ASCVD with CKD and inflammation

Bottom lineDownstream IL-6 blockade does not reduce events in this high-risk CKD population, and it adds infections. Colchicine is still the inflammation option, but this result argues against IL-6 as a target in CKD. Funded by Novo Nordisk.

Design
Double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT; N about 6,376; median 33 months
Population
ASCVD, CKD (mean eGFR 44.5) and hs-CRP ≥2 mg/L; mean age 69, 66% diabetes, 42% HF
Comparison
Ziltivekimab 15 mg SC monthly vs placebo
Primary endpoint
CV death, MI or stroke 17.6% vs 17.7%, HR 0.99 (0.88–1.11), despite hs-CRP −64 and free IL-6 −76 points vs placebo
Safety
Serious infections 20.4% vs 17.9%; cellulitis 2.9% vs 1.0%; hospitalization for infection 17.1% vs 14.8%
NEJM · Nov 7, 2026 · PMID pending

OPTIMAL-DIABETES: systolic target below 120 vs below 140 mm Hg in type 2 diabetes

Bottom lineIn type 2 diabetes, pushing systolic BP below 120 mm Hg gave no significant MACE reduction and caused more kidney-function decline. Keep the <130 mm Hg target. Event rates were lower than expected, which limits power.

Design
Open-label RCT at 33 sites in Brazil; N=9,476; median 4 years
Population
Type 2 diabetes, age ≥50, SBP 130–180 mm Hg plus CVD, subclinical CVD, CKD or other risk factors; mean age 65, 62% women
Comparison
Target <120 vs <140 mm Hg; achieved about 122 vs 135 mm Hg
Primary endpoint
CV death, stroke, MI, or hospitalization for HF or unstable angina 6.0% vs 6.7%; about 10% lower, not significant
Safety
Kidney events (mainly eGFR decline) 5.7% vs 3.7%; ESKD and serious AEs similar
NEJM · Nov 7, 2026 · PMID pending

STREAM: stopping primary-prevention statins in multimorbid adults aged 70 and over

Bottom lineTechnically non-inferior, but stopping meant about 1 extra MI, stroke or death per 40 patients over 2 years, with no gain in symptoms or quality of life. This does not support routine statin deprescribing; reserve it for limited life expectancy or patient preference after an explicit discussion.

Design
Open-label non-inferiority RCT with blinded adjudication; 24 sites in Switzerland, the Netherlands and France; N=1,880; median 103 weeks
Population
Age ≥70 (mean 77.5), two or more chronic conditions, no known ASCVD, on a statin ≥1 year; 47% women
Comparison
Stopping vs continuing the statin
Primary endpoint
MI, ischaemic stroke or death as 2-year restricted mean event-free time 96.5 vs 98.5 weeks (−2.0, −3.7 to −0.3); non-inferior against a −5.2-week margin
Full follow-up
Events 12.7% vs 9.2%, HR 1.38 (1.05–1.81)
Safety
No difference in quality of life, pain, falls or sarcopenia; LDL-C 176 vs 99 mg/dL at 12 months (subsample)
JAMA · Nov 7, 2026 · PMID 42998244

ISCHEMIA-EXTEND: 11-year survival after invasive vs conservative management of chronic coronary disease

Bottom lineRoutine invasive management of stable ischaemia does not prolong life, even at 11 years. Revascularize for angina that is not controlled by medical therapy, and for high-risk anatomy outside ISCHEMIA's criteria. Companion papers agree: in the COURAGE veterans cohort, 25-year death was 83.9% with PCI vs 82.2% (HR 0.98, 0.85–1.13; JAMA), and ISCHEMIA-CKD EXTEND found 8-year death of 57.4% vs 56.8% (AHR 1.05; Circulation).

Design
Extended follow-up of the ISCHEMIA RCT; 320 sites in 37 countries; N=5,179; median 9 years (to 13.4)
Population
Moderate or severe ischaemia, LVEF ≥35%, no left main disease, unacceptable angina or recent ACS
Comparison
Initial invasive strategy plus GDMT vs GDMT with invasive management for failure of medical therapy
Primary endpoint
All-cause death at 11 years 25.8% vs 26.9% (−1.0 point, −4.2 to 2.1); adjusted HR 0.97 (0.86–1.09)
Cause of death
CV death 15.8% vs 17.1% (AHR 0.88, 0.75–1.02), offset by non-CV death 9.1% vs 8.1%
Circulation · Nov 7, 2026 · PMID 42998308

eZ-PAVE: initial statin–ezetimibe vs statin monotherapy within LDL-C treat-to-target strategies

Bottom lineWhen LDL-C is actively treated to target, starting with combination therapy rather than titrating up gave no extra event benefit over 3 years. Reaching the target is what matters. Open-label, with a small LDL-C gap between arms.

Design
Prespecified second randomization (2×2) in the Ez-PAVE RCT; 17 Korean centres; N=3,048; 3 years
Population
ASCVD, randomized to LDL-C targets <55 vs <70 mg/dL
Comparison
Initial statin–ezetimibe vs statin monotherapy, titrated to target
Primary endpoint
CV death, MI, stroke, revascularization or UA hospitalization 8.5% vs 7.9%, HR 1.07 (0.83–1.37)
LDL-C
Median 53 vs 62 mg/dL (intensive) and 63 vs 70 mg/dL (conventional); ezetimibe added later in 25–34% of the monotherapy arm

NEJM · Nov 5, 2026 · PMID 42981569Practice-changing

SHASTA-3 and SHASTA-4: plozasiran for severe hypertriglyceridaemia

Bottom lineThe first APOC3 therapy to show fewer pancreatitis episodes in the broader severe-HTG population, not only in familial chylomicronaemia; expect an indication expansion. Pancreatitis was a pooled analysis with few events, and there are no CV outcome data yet. Funded by Arrowhead.

Design
Two identical phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCTs, 2:1; N=757 pooled; 12 months
Population
Adults with severe hypertriglyceridaemia
Comparison
Plozasiran 25 mg SC at baseline and months 3, 6 and 9 vs placebo
Primary endpoint
Fasting TG at 12 months −79% and −81% from baseline; placebo-adjusted −45 (−51 to −38) and −47 (−54 to −39) points. TG <500 mg/dL in 91–93% vs 50–51%
Pancreatitis
1 vs 5 per 100 patient-years; rate ratio 0.22, p=0.008
Safety
Adverse events 73% in both groups
Lancet · Nov 7, 2026 · PMID 42998670

DAPT-SHOCK-AMI: cangrelor vs crushed ticagrelor in MI with cardiogenic shock

Bottom lineCangrelor gives reliable platelet inhibition in shock, where oral absorption fails, but no clinical advantage was shown; with a 1-point margin the trial was not built to show one. Reasonable when oral loading is impractical (intubated or vomiting patients); there is no case for routine use.

Design
Double-blind RCT at 29 hospitals in 5 European countries; N=605; 30 days
Population
AMI complicated by cardiogenic shock undergoing primary PCI
Comparison
IV cangrelor (bolus plus infusion ≥2 h) vs crushed ticagrelor 180 mg
Primary endpoints
Platelet reactivity <50% at end of PCI 100% vs 22% (lab subset, n=124). Death, MI or ischaemic stroke at 30 days 38% vs 41% (−4 points, 90% CI −10 to 3); non-inferiority not shown
Safety
Major bleeding 6% vs 5%

Eur Heart J · Nov 4, 2026 · PMID 42978512

H-HeFT: hydralazine–isosorbide dinitrate in contemporary HFrEF

Bottom lineAdding H-ISDN to modern GDMT in mostly White patients with HFrEF does not reduce HF events or death. Keep it for self-identified Black patients (A-HeFT) and for patients who cannot take RAAS inhibitors. Small and underpowered for mortality.

Design
Double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT at 23 Danish hospitals; N=592; mean 3.7 years
Population
HFrEF (LVEF ≤40%), NYHA II–IV, raised NT-proBNP, on contemporary therapy; median age 72, 18% women
Comparison
H-ISDN titrated to 75/40 mg three times daily vs placebo
Primary endpoint
Worsening HF, transplant, LVAD or death 30.6% vs 30.5%, HR 0.90 (0.67–1.21)
Secondary
Deaths 57 vs 72 (HR 0.72, 0.51–1.02); worsening HF events HR 1.01

Circulation · Nov 7, 2026 · PMID 42998297

LATENT: AI-ECG-guided screening for atrial fibrillation

Bottom lineAI-ECG finds the patients most likely to have AF on monitoring, but at population level the yield was tiny, and confirmed AF or flutter detection (0.3% vs 0.2%) and treatment did not change. No case yet for AI-ECG-triggered screening outside trials.

Design
Single-centre, open-label pragmatic RCT; N=14,665; 90 days
Population
Adults aged 40–85 without known AF or flutter
Comparison
AI-ECG review of prior sinus-rhythm ECGs, with up to 7-day Holter for high-risk flags (1.1%), vs usual care with matched controls monitored the same way
Primary endpoint
New AF, flutter or pre-AF atrial findings 0.74% vs 0.48% (OR 1.54, 1.01–2.37; p=0.046); reclassifying one event removes significance. High-risk group 32.1% vs 2.6%
Safety
Monitoring-related skin reactions or discomfort 22.9% vs 18.8%
Europace · Nov 4, 2026 · PMID 42980655

HOBIT-AF: structured weight loss after AF ablation in obesity

Bottom lineAfter a successful ablation in mostly paroxysmal AF, modest weight loss improved metabolic markers but not AF recurrence; low post-ablation burden left little room to show an effect. Weight loss is still worth doing for its own sake. Small trial.

Design
RCT; N=167; intervention from 3 to 18 months after ablation
Population
Obesity (mean BMI 35.6) after catheter ablation, mostly paroxysmal AF; mean age 59
Comparison
Comprehensive weight-loss programme vs usual care
Primary endpoint
Change in AF/AT burden on 14-day Holter +0.6% vs −0.9%; no difference. Weight loss 6.8% in the active arm
Secondary
Arrhythmia-free survival 70.4% vs 69.6%
